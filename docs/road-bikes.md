## Scope and comparison conditions

These are standard production-road configurations. All five use the existing shared solver, instantaneous
automatic shifts, launch assistance, torque protections and 0.9 driveline efficiency. Those assists are game
behavior, not claims that the real machines had automatic transmission, ABS or traction control. No new
physics, speed correction, vehicle-specific power multiplier, course or series is introduced. All five use
ordinary contact and recovery; none opts into the Lotus SPECIAL's contact explosion.

The comparison occupant is 75 kg plus 5 kg of helmet, clothing and equipment. Fuel is fixed at 10 litres,
using an explicitly estimated density of 0.75 kg/litre. A source's dry or wet mass is kept separate from
this run condition; existing fluids or fuel are not counted twice. Fuel consumption is not simulated.
Suspension uses the current common provisional bike calibration: 1.5 Hz ride frequency, 0.4 damping ratio
and 0.3 m travel. CG height is wheelbase × 0.3, including the whole run mass; none of these is a measured
period suspension or mass-distribution claim.

The numeric ledger distinguishes `source verified`, `calculated`, `engineering estimate` and `game design`.
Calculations retain their inputs' uncertainty. Original crankshaft power, rear-wheel measurements and units
remain distinct. Runtime vehicle JSON is the sole numerical authority; the CSV is an audit, not a second loader.

Catalog membership makes each bike selectable in FREE PLAY and eligible for its existing BIKES/ALL rival pools.

## Provisional brake calibration

GPZ900R and GSX-R750 brake capacities are game-design calibration, not verified period torque measurements.
The existing VFR750R provides a common reference: 700/300 Nm at 273.5 kg and front/rear rolling radii
0.2999/0.3306 m. Preserve each station's nominal brake force per loaded mass by calculating
`referenceTorque × (mass / 273.5) × (radius / referenceRadius)`, then round to 50 Nm.
This gives GPZ900R 800/350 Nm and GSX-R750 700/300 Nm. Their unbounded brake demand is
11.882/11.940 m/s², close to the VFR's 11.852 m/s²; actual forces still obey the shared tire/load protection.

The steady braking planner does not reproduce every suspension/pitch transient. The initial unsupported
capacity estimates strongly unloaded the rear tire and exceeded the existing 5% plan/solver agreement gate.
The common-reference calibration reduces that mismatch without changing wheel inertia, verified geometry,
engine output, tire laws, assistance or the test tolerance. Other road-bike capacities already have lower nominal
force per loaded mass and remain as documented. No historical hydraulic pressure or stopping distance is inferred
from this game calibration.

## Model limits

V-Boost is represented once in the VMAX's full-throttle steady torque curve. Its transient valve movement and
partial-throttle behavior are not simulated. The shaft bike uses the same shared driveline efficiency as the
chain bikes; shaft reactions and separate transmission loss models are absent. Tire temperature, wear,
fuel consumption, clutch heat and real shift duration are outside this addition.

Bank images describe visual lean samples, not measured cornering limits. Step contact, period tire carcass
behavior, chassis flex and individual suspension geometry are not reconstructed. The two contact stations
and common protection rules limit comparisons with period braking, acceleration and handling tests.
Provisional geometry and acoustic dimensions are reproducible approximations, not dimensioned factory models
or recordings of the actual machines.

Both sound definitions use two crank revolutions per cycle, with evenly spaced events at 0, 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 in the source firing order 1-2-4-3. This is the conventional four-stroke inline-four timing interpretation; no two-stroke engine definition is reused. The four primary branches are indexed in firing-event order. Their estimated pairing, balance connection, internal silencer geometry and lengths/diameters are acoustic approximations of a standard dual-outlet system, not measured tube metrology or sampled recordings.

The Suzuki equivalent valve diameter is `sqrt(2)*23 mm`, from two source-verified 23 mm exhaust valves. Honda uses the same assumed individual diameter, explicitly unverified for this model. Honda's open fraction uses source zero-lift timing `(93 BBDC +180 +70 ATDC)/720 =343/720`; the separate one-mm timing is not substituted. Suzuki's 288/720 open fraction is estimated. Constant effective valve opening and straight-tube mufflers simplify the source hardware. Sound synthesis continues to use the common engine RPM/load state; none of these acoustic values modifies engine power.

Two crank revolutions per cycle, four cylinders. The **manual's stroke diagram**, rather than an inference from V4 alone, supplies chronological phases 0/720, 180/720, 430/720, 610/720 (cylinders 1, 3, 2, 4). Intervals are 180°, 250°, 180°, 110°. Header lengths 0.34/0.36/0.80/0.82 m and diameters 32 mm are estimates; exact cylinder-to-acoustic-length association and internal chamber graph are unverified. These are synthesized acoustic approximations, not recordings of the actual bike.

## Provisional pictures

The generator is `npm run road-bike-pictures -- <vehicle ID|all> [replace]`. It admits the authored mechanics document and uses the common procedural surface sampler, vehicle picture baker and camera. No game solver, runtime renderer, display control or vehicle-specific camera is introduced. Existing racing IDs are never in the road-bike CLI's target list.

Each set has 64 yaw directions, seven evenly spaced banks from -45 to +45 degrees, six independently drawn perspective sizes, a shadow for every frame, and stock/red/blue/black palettes. Only the stock scheme is a historical selection. The other three are game colors, with a visibly different game red from the GPZ's stock red and a different game black from the CB's stock black. No claim of four factory colors is made. Anonymous road riders are upright on CB900F and VMAX and inclined forward on the three sports bikes. The rider's visual height can exceed the manufacturer's vehicle-only overall height. The image range is authored at ±45° so that tires remain the support surface under the shared ground fit; it does not establish a physical lean, cornering or ground-clearance limit. The source constant owns both geometry angles and sprite metadata. All 35 bank poses were checked: a tire is the support surface in every pose, both tires contact at neutral, and banked front-tire clearance ranges from 0 to 12.1 mm (less than half a native texel), reflecting the different tire widths under the shared ground fit. [ground-check.json](road-bikes/ground-check.json) records each pose.

Material tags authored on the shape feed the shared paint/glass/brake-lamp classifications. Tail lamps use reserved slot 15 and declared dark-red tail/brighter-red brake colors; headlights and amber indicators are fixed appearance. Indicators are not newly simulated controls. Metals, tires, lamps and the rider keep their colors across palette choices. Actual left/right geometry is sampled independently; no mirror-copying generates opposing yaw views.

Body details are authored estimates from actual viewed references, not traced images or manufacturer CAD. Reference pixels are not included in game assets. Mirrors, fenders, cast wheels and separate front/rear rim diameters are present. Chains are on the left; VMAX has its left shaft housing/final case and dished 15-inch rear wheel. CB and KATANA have paired rear shocks; GPZ and GSX-R use the visual single-shock layout. All except GSX-R have two stock-style silencers; GSX-R has a single right-side outlet. CB has the naked round headlight and black NH1 body with red/orange bands; KATANA has its silver rectangular-light nose; GPZ has a red/gray full fairing and rectangular light; GSX-R has its 1985 white/blue pattern, blue seat/nose and paired round lights; VMAX has a maroon upper cover, silver intake-look and rear side covers, round lamp, low seat and V4 block.

Shared authoring fix: if every texel of a projected silhouette falls below the ordinary half-coverage threshold, retain the most-covered texel with its ordinary palette classification. Equal coverage keeps the first texel in row order. Frames already containing an opaque texel are untouched. This is a common picture-baking rule, not a road-bike or camera exception. Existing saved sprites were not regenerated; freshly regenerating earlier racing bikes would now make their previously empty far-LOD cells visible. The semantic sampler's default geometry/colors/masks were checked byte-for-byte on all seven existing NSR banks before/after extension. All five existing full-size racing PNGs were also reproduced byte-for-byte by the extracted contact-sheet helper.

The generic regression verifies that a very narrow painted or lamp surface remains visible through all six sizes, retains its own palette class and lamp colors, shares an integral anchor grid with its shadow, and regenerates deterministically.

PNG layout: each `<sprite>-lodN.png` has banks in declaration order, and 64 yaw directions within each bank, eight per row. `overview.png` stacks the five bikes in catalog order, eight yaw directions at neutral bank. `banks.png` stacks all seven banks for each bike with eight yaw directions. `palettes-and-lamps.png` stacks stock/red/blue/black, tail then brake for each color, eight yaw directions, for each bike in catalog order. `review.json` records per-set hashes and per-LOD transparency/lamp counts. All contact sheets preserve native texel size.

Provisional limitations: sponsor/manufacturer lettering, exact spoke sections, badge shapes, detailed instruments, tread grooves and articulated hand/finger geometry are omitted. Paint RGB values approximate viewed references; no unverified factory color code is invented. Exhaust and fork/engine geometry are visual approximations. GPZ front/rear brake discs use provisional 280/270 mm secondary-source diameters; GSX-R uses 300/280 mm. GPZ retains the provisional 38 mm fork diameter despite conflicting secondary 39 mm reports; this is a visual authoring choice, not a suspension-data change. No real step-contact or maximum-bank limit is simulated.
